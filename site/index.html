About Rave Monitor

Rave Monitor maps electronic music in real time: club nights, festivals, concerts and where touring DJs play next. It's made by Geff Bohm.

It started inside split/shift, a Milwaukee dance-music nonprofit, out of a need for a better way to browse events and happenings, so we could plan our work and create new event experiences with the whole scene in view. Now it's open source and free for everyone.

It's free to use, with every feature open to everyone. If it's useful to you, you can , once or every month, to keep it running.

The archive

Every listing is kept after the night is over, merged across platforms, so this grows into a living archive of the scene. Search the archive by artist, record label, venue, city and month, or download it as a SQLite database or a spreadsheet (CSV).

Visits are counted with Google Analytics, with no ad features.

Searches on the map are counted with nothing about who searched. Rave Monitor may share or sell trends, like the most-followed artists in a city, only for groups of 10 or more people and never anything about you.

Near me uses your location only inside your browser, to move the map. It's never sent anywhere. Until you press it, the map starts from a guess based on your time zone.

Where listings come from

  • Scrapers on a schedule. Ready-made Apify scrapers pull public listings from Resident Advisor, Posh, Partiful and Luma once a month.
  • Free APIs. Ticketmaster Discovery (free key, 5,000 calls a day) and Skiddle (free key, strong on UK club nights).
  • Livestreams. NTS Radio's public schedule, read live in your browser, and YouTube's API for live and scheduled streams on channels like Boiler Room, Cercle and HÖR.
  • One feed. Every source is converted to one event format, duplicates across platforms are merged, and each listing links back to its source page.
  • Event details only. Title, time, venue, lineup, price and link. No attendee lists or organizer contact details.
  • Open source, for fun. A non-commercial proof of concept. The code is public; the scraped data is rebuilt by each deployment rather than stored in the repo.
